# 1.7 Enterprise Operations Architecture

## Purpose

The Enterprise Operations Architecture defines how ACOS is operated safely, reliably, measurably and continuously across production and non-production environments.

## Operating principles

1. Reliability is a product capability and must be designed, measured and governed.
2. Every production service has an accountable owner, service tier, SLOs, runbooks and escalation path.
3. Changes are risk-classified before deployment and remain observable after deployment.
4. Incidents are managed through a consistent command model with evidence-preserving timelines.
5. Repeated incidents trigger problem management and permanent corrective action.
6. Recovery capabilities are tested; untested recovery plans are treated as assumptions.
7. Operational controls fail closed for critical security, data integrity and financial risks.

## Operational domains

### Service reliability

Services are assigned a service tier that determines minimum availability, support coverage, recovery objectives, deployment safeguards and incident response expectations.

### Release and deployment

Production changes use controlled promotion, automated validation, progressive delivery where appropriate, rollback or roll-forward procedures and post-deployment verification.

### Incident management

Incidents are classified by severity, assigned an incident commander and tracked from detection through stabilization, recovery, review and corrective action.

### Change management

Changes are classified as standard, normal or emergency. Approval depth and evidence requirements increase with risk and blast radius.

### Problem management

Recurring or high-impact failures produce problem records, root-cause analysis, corrective actions, accountable owners and due dates.

### Business continuity

Critical services define recovery time objectives, recovery point objectives, dependency recovery order, alternate operating procedures and exercise cadence.

### Operational observability

Operations depends on correlated metrics, logs, traces, audit records, deployment markers, synthetic checks and business-level health indicators.

## Control model

The machine-readable operations registry is the source of truth for service tiers, SLOs, incident severities, change classes, runbooks, escalation paths, continuity plans and operational invariants.

The registry is validated in CI against `schemas/operations-registry.schema.json` and semantic invariants enforced by `scripts/validate_operations_registry.py`.

## Required invariants

- Every service references an existing service tier.
- Every production service defines at least one SLO and one runbook.
- Critical services define both RTO and RPO.
- Every runbook has an owner and review cadence.
- Every incident severity defines response and escalation targets.
- Emergency changes require retrospective review.
- SLO objectives are within the range 0 < objective <= 100.
- Recovery objectives are positive durations.

## Evidence and audit

Operational decisions, overrides, emergency changes, incident role assignments, recovery tests and post-incident actions must be traceable to immutable evidence.
