# 1.5 Enterprise Platform Architecture

## Purpose

The ACOS platform architecture defines the shared runtime, identity, integration, data, observability, security and delivery capabilities that support all enterprise domains without owning their business semantics.

## Platform principles

1. Domain services own business rules and authoritative data.
2. Platform services provide reusable technical capabilities through stable contracts.
3. Every production action is authenticated, authorized, observable and auditable.
4. Secrets never enter source control, logs, prompts or event payloads.
5. Production changes are immutable, reproducible and progressively delivered.
6. Failure isolation, backpressure and graceful degradation are mandatory.
7. Multi-tenant boundaries are explicit and enforced at every layer.
8. Infrastructure is policy governed and continuously validated.

## Platform capability groups

### Identity and trust

- workforce and workload identity
- service-to-service authentication
- short-lived credentials
- authorization and policy decision points
- tenant and environment isolation
- certificate and key lifecycle management

### Runtime and orchestration

- stateless compute
- durable workflow execution
- scheduled and event-driven workloads
- workload admission control
- autoscaling and resource quotas
- health, readiness and liveness management

### Messaging and integration

- command and event transport
- schema-governed contracts
- transactional outbox support
- dead-letter handling
- delivery guarantees and replay controls
- provider anti-corruption gateways

### Data platform

- transactional persistence
- object storage
- cache and distributed coordination
- analytics and read models
- encryption, retention and lineage
- backup, restore and disaster recovery

### Observability and operations

- structured logs
- distributed traces
- metrics and service-level indicators
- audit evidence
- incident detection and response
- runbooks, error budgets and operational ownership

### Delivery and supply chain

- source integrity
- dependency and artifact provenance
- build, test and security gates
- environment promotion
- progressive delivery and rollback
- configuration and feature-flag governance

## Reference topology

ACOS uses a layered topology:

1. Edge and access plane
2. Identity and policy plane
3. Application runtime plane
4. Workflow and messaging plane
5. Data plane
6. Observability and audit plane
7. Delivery and control plane

No application workload may bypass the identity, policy, telemetry or audit controls associated with its environment.

## Environment model

The canonical environments are development, test, staging and production. Production data and credentials are isolated from non-production. Promotion occurs through signed artifacts and declarative configuration; source rebuilding during promotion is prohibited.

## Reliability model

Every registered platform service declares:

- criticality tier
- availability objective
- recovery time objective
- recovery point objective
- scaling mode
- dependency set
- degradation mode
- backup and restore policy
- operational owner

Critical services must avoid single-zone dependency and must define tested recovery procedures.

## Security model

The platform adopts zero-trust principles. Access is denied by default. Workloads receive least-privilege permissions using short-lived identities. Network location alone never grants trust. Sensitive operations require explicit policy evaluation and produce immutable audit records.

## Data protection

Data classification controls encryption, retention, replication, export and deletion. Customer and regulated data must be encrypted in transit and at rest. Data movement across domain or regional boundaries requires an approved contract and lineage record.

## Deployment model

Deployments use immutable artifacts, environment-specific configuration and progressive rollout. Supported strategies include rolling, blue-green and canary deployment. Automated rollback is required when defined health thresholds are breached.

## Platform invariants

- No long-lived production credential in application configuration.
- No unregistered production service or data store.
- No direct cross-domain database access.
- No production deployment without provenance and validation evidence.
- No critical dependency without timeout, retry and circuit-breaking policy.
- No deletion of audit evidence before its retention period expires.
- No recovery objective without an executable and tested recovery procedure.
