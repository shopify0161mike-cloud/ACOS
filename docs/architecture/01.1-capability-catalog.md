# 1.1 Enterprise Capability Catalog

## Purpose

The Enterprise Capability Catalog provides the human-readable view of the canonical machine-readable capability registry. It defines how ACOS capabilities are identified, governed, owned, measured and connected to implementation without allowing documentation to drift into a second source of truth.

The canonical data source is:

```text
config/capabilities/registry.yaml
```

The canonical schema is:

```text
schemas/capability-registry.schema.json
```

## Capability definition

A capability is a stable enterprise ability that ACOS must possess to produce a governed business or platform outcome. A capability is not a screen, workflow, prompt, vendor integration, microservice, team or project.

Capabilities remain stable while implementations may change.

## Identifier convention

Capability identifiers use the following pattern:

```text
CAP-<DOMAIN-GROUP>-<DOMAIN-CODE>-<SEQUENCE>
```

Examples:

```text
CAP-COM-PRO-001
CAP-INT-EXE-001
CAP-GOV-PLY-001
CAP-PLT-RUN-001
```

Domain groups:

| Code | Domain group | Responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| `COM` | Commerce | Commercial value creation and customer operations |
| `INT` | Intelligence | Evidence interpretation, recommendations and governed coordination |
| `GOV` | Governance | Policy, approval, risk, security, budget and audit control |
| `PLT` | Platform | Identity, runtime, events, integrations, knowledge and observability |

## Mandatory capability metadata

Every capability entry must define at least:

- immutable capability ID and name;
- domain group and domain;
- purpose;
- business and technical ownership;
- criticality and maturity;
- lifecycle state;
- authority level;
- security classification;
- AI enablement status;
- capability dependencies;
- governing policies;
- emitted and consumed events;
- measurable outcomes;
- owned data;
- recovery objectives;
- acceptance criteria;
- implementation references.

## Authority levels

| Authority level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `analyze` | May interpret evidence without creating material side effects |
| `recommend` | May produce a governed recommendation for an authorized decision-maker |
| `execute_within_authority` | May execute only within explicitly delegated policy, budget and risk limits |
| `coordinate_autonomy` | May coordinate multiple governed capabilities but cannot expand its own authority |
| `human_exclusive` | Requires attributable human authority; AI cannot perform the decision |

Authority is capability-specific and cannot be inferred from AI enablement alone.

## Criticality

| Level | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| `C1` | Low business impact; tolerant of extended interruption |
| `C2` | Material supporting capability |
| `C3` | High-impact operational capability |
| `C4` | Enterprise-critical, governance-critical or safety-critical capability |

Criticality controls the required rigor for recovery, audit, validation, release and change governance.

## Lifecycle

The governed lifecycle is defined in [Capability Lifecycle](diagrams/capability-lifecycle.mmd).

Canonical states include:

```text
proposed
architecture_review
approved
planned
implementing
validated
operational
observed
improving
deprecated
retired
rejected
```

A lifecycle transition is a governed state change, not a documentation label. Evidence must exist for every material transition.

## Dependency model

Dependencies represent capabilities that must exist or provide an outcome before another capability can meet its own acceptance criteria.

Dependency rules:

- every reference must resolve to a registered capability;
- self-dependencies are prohibited;
- duplicate dependencies are prohibited;
- circular dependencies are prohibited;
- dependencies must not be used to bypass domain boundaries or governance gates;
- runtime interaction still requires explicit contracts, even when a dependency exists.

The canonical dependency visualization is maintained in [Capability Dependency Graph](diagrams/capability-dependency-graph.mmd).

## Governance model

The mandatory governance sequence is defined in [Capability Governance Flow](diagrams/capability-governance-flow.mmd).

Material side effects require, as applicable:

1. trusted context and identity;
2. registry and lifecycle resolution;
3. policy evaluation;
4. risk assessment;
5. budget and exposure validation;
6. authority resolution;
7. human approval for human-exclusive decisions;
8. runtime precondition checks;
9. durable and idempotent execution;
10. postcondition validation;
11. immutable audit evidence;
12. correlated observability and outcome recording.

## Domain group summary

### Commerce capabilities

Commerce capabilities create and operate customer and commercial value. Current registry scope includes product discovery and qualification, supplier discovery and qualification, unit economics, brand identity, campaign planning, order lifecycle and fulfillment coordination.

### Intelligence capabilities

Intelligence capabilities convert governed evidence into recommendations and coordinated plans. They may not create authority, modify the Constitution or bypass runtime gates.

### Governance capabilities

Governance capabilities define and enforce policy, approval, risk, budget, identity and audit constraints. Sensitive failures must fail closed.

### Platform capabilities

Platform capabilities provide reusable execution, eventing, integration, identity, knowledge and operational foundations. Business policy must not be embedded in provider adapters or workflow definitions.

## Implementation linkage

The `implementation_refs` field links architecture to migrations, services, modules, APIs, workflows or infrastructure that realize a capability.

Implementation references:

- are traceability links, not ownership transfers;
- must not redefine the capability purpose or authority;
- must be versioned or otherwise uniquely identifiable;
- should be added only when the implementation exists or has an approved specification;
- must be removed or updated when an implementation is retired or replaced.

## Maintenance rule

Changes to capability meaning, ownership, authority, dependencies, lifecycle, policy linkage, data ownership, events, criticality or acceptance criteria require an update to the registry and may require an ADR.

Editorial documentation may summarize the registry, but must never contradict it.
