# 1.5 Platform Reliability and Security Model

## Service criticality

- Tier 0: identity, policy, event and workflow services whose loss prevents safe enterprise operation.
- Tier 1: observability, delivery and shared data services required for dependable operation.
- Tier 2: productivity and optimization services with bounded business impact.
- Tier 3: experimental or non-critical services.

Criticality determines minimum availability, recovery testing, deployment strategy, alerting and escalation requirements.

## Service-level management

Each production service must define service-level indicators and an availability objective. Error-budget consumption controls release velocity. A service that exhausts its error budget prioritizes reliability work over non-essential feature delivery until risk returns to an approved level.

## Resilience controls

All remote dependencies require explicit timeouts. Retries are bounded, use backoff and jitter, and are permitted only for classified transient failures. Circuit breakers prevent cascading failure. Bulkheads isolate tenants, workloads or dependency groups where material blast-radius reduction is possible.

## Recovery controls

Recovery time and recovery point objectives must be measurable and supported by backups, replication or rebuild procedures. Recovery tests must validate both technical restoration and business-level correctness. Untested backups do not count as a recovery control.

## Identity and access

Human and workload identities are separate. Production workloads use short-lived credentials and narrowly scoped authorization. Privileged access is time-bound, approved and audited. Break-glass access requires a declared incident, independent review and post-event evidence.

## Secrets and cryptography

Secrets are sourced from a managed secrets service or workload identity. Rotation is automated where supported. Encryption keys have named owners, lifecycle policies and revocation procedures. Secrets and sensitive tokens must be redacted from telemetry.

## Software supply chain

Production artifacts must be traceable to source, build identity, dependency set and validation evidence. Artifacts are immutable after build. Promotion reuses the same artifact across environments. Critical vulnerabilities, missing provenance or failed policy gates block production promotion.

## Network and tenant isolation

Network policy follows least privilege. Public ingress terminates at approved edge controls. Internal services are not made public by default. Tenant identity is propagated and verified at service and data boundaries; tenant context supplied solely by an untrusted caller is insufficient.

## Incident operations

Every Tier 0 and Tier 1 service has an operational owner, alert routing, severity model and runbook. Incident records preserve timeline, impact, decisions and remediation. Significant incidents result in blameless review and tracked corrective actions.

## Mandatory evidence

The platform must retain evidence for deployments, privileged access, policy decisions, recovery tests, security scans, incident response and configuration changes according to governance retention requirements.
