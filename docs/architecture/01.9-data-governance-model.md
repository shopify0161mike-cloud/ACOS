# 1.9 Data Governance Model

## Governance roles

- **Business owner**: accountable for purpose, lawful use, value and semantic correctness.
- **Technical owner**: accountable for implementation, reliability, contract delivery and recovery.
- **Data steward**: accountable for metadata, quality, classification, lineage and lifecycle evidence.
- **Data custodian**: operates storage, backup, encryption and access mechanisms.
- **Consumer owner**: ensures use remains compatible with the published contract and approved purpose.

## Decision rights

The producing domain controls meaning and contract evolution. Enterprise governance controls mandatory classification, privacy, retention, security and audit rules. Platform authorities control shared storage and processing standards. Intelligence governance approves data used by models, agents, retrieval and memory systems.

## Required controls

Every active data product must provide:

1. Stable identifier and purpose.
2. Business owner, technical owner and steward.
3. Classification and access policy.
4. Authoritative source and storage class.
5. Versioned contract and compatibility mode.
6. Quality objectives for completeness, validity, freshness and availability.
7. Retention and deletion policy.
8. Source-to-consumer lineage.
9. Encryption and masking requirements.
10. Recovery objectives and backup class.

## Change governance

- Compatible additive changes follow normal review and automated validation.
- Breaking semantic or structural changes require a major contract version.
- Breaking changes require a migration plan, named consumers, deprecation window and rollback path.
- Restricted-data changes require security and privacy review.
- AI-use changes require provenance and intended-use review.

## Quality governance

Quality failures must be observable, assigned and time-bounded. Active products that fall below objectives must publish impact, affected consumers and remediation status. Repeated failures trigger architecture or operational review.

## Retention and deletion

Retention is based on business purpose, legal requirements and minimization. Deletion must cascade to governed replicas and derived indexes where policy requires it. Legal hold overrides normal deletion while preserving an auditable authority record.

## Non-production use

Production confidential or restricted data may not enter development or test environments without approved masking, anonymization, tokenization or synthetic replacement.

## AI and knowledge governance

Documents, chunks, embeddings, features, prompts, memories, evaluations and feedback datasets must retain provenance and policy alignment. Retrieval indexes are derived assets and must honor source classification, access and deletion rules.

## Evidence

Registry history, schema validation, change review, quality results, restore tests, access decisions and deletion records form the minimum evidence set for data governance assurance.
