# 1.7 SRE Operating Model

## Mission

The SRE operating model converts reliability objectives into daily operational practice. It aligns product teams, platform teams and incident responders around measurable service health and controlled operational risk.

## Service ownership

Each service has one accountable service owner and one technical owner. Ownership includes SLO definition, on-call readiness, runbook quality, dependency visibility, recovery testing and corrective-action completion.

## Service tiers

- **tier-0-critical**: enterprise-critical control plane or irreversible business impact; 24x7 response and tested continuity required.
- **tier-1-essential**: essential production service with significant customer or operational impact.
- **tier-2-standard**: standard production capability with business-hours ownership and documented recovery.
- **tier-3-internal**: internal or non-critical capability with proportionate controls.

## SLO and error-budget practice

SLIs measure user-visible or business-relevant outcomes. SLOs define the target reliability over a fixed window. Error budgets govern release velocity and operational intervention.

When error-budget consumption breaches a defined threshold, teams must reduce change risk, prioritize reliability work and document the recovery plan.

## Incident command

A major incident uses explicit roles:

- Incident Commander
- Operations Lead
- Communications Lead
- Subject Matter Experts
- Scribe

The Incident Commander owns coordination and decisions, not every technical action.

## Change safety

Changes are evaluated by service tier, reversibility, data risk, security risk, blast radius and dependency impact. Progressive delivery and automated rollback are preferred for high-risk runtime changes.

## Problem management

A problem record is mandatory for severe incidents, repeated failures, breached recovery objectives or material control failures. Corrective actions must be specific, owned, time-bound and verifiable.

## Toil management

Recurring manual operational work is measured. High-volume, repetitive and automatable toil is prioritized for elimination when it threatens reliability, response quality or engineering capacity.

## Continuity and exercises

Critical recovery procedures are exercised on a defined cadence. Exercises validate people, permissions, dependencies, backups, communication channels and decision authority.

## Operational reviews

Teams conduct recurring service reviews covering SLO performance, incidents, change failure rate, recovery readiness, open risks, dependency health and overdue actions.
