# 1.3 Saga and Compensation Architecture

Status: Canonical

## Purpose

This document defines how ACOS coordinates multi-step, cross-domain work without distributed database transactions. Sagas preserve domain autonomy while providing durable progress, controlled compensation and complete evidence.

## Saga Model

A saga is an ordered graph of governed steps. Each step declares:

- owning domain;
- command contract;
- preconditions;
- authorization reference;
- idempotency scope;
- timeout and retry policy;
- success event;
- compensating command where one exists;
- irreversibility classification;
- evidence requirements.

## Coordination Rules

- The Runtime Domain owns saga coordination state.
- Business state remains owned by the participating domains.
- A saga may dispatch commands but may not directly mutate domain persistence.
- Every step completion is durably recorded before the next dependent step is released.
- Forward progress and compensation use the same governance, identity, idempotency and audit requirements.

## Compensation Classes

### Reversible

The forward effect can be safely neutralized through a deterministic compensating command, such as releasing a reservation.

### Conditionally Reversible

Compensation is possible only while explicit preconditions hold, such as cancelling an order before fulfillment begins.

### Mitigatable

The original effect cannot be undone, but exposure can be reduced through a separate action, such as pausing a campaign after publication.

### Irreversible

No safe automated reversal exists. Execution requires explicit approval, containment procedures and a manual recovery plan before the step begins.

## Compensation Ordering

Compensation normally occurs in reverse dependency order, not merely reverse start time. Parallel branches may compensate independently only when their state and dependencies permit it.

## Compensation Safety

Before dispatching compensation, the runtime must:

1. verify current authoritative state;
2. confirm the compensating command remains valid;
3. re-evaluate policy and authority where required;
4. use a dedicated idempotency key;
5. persist compensation intent;
6. execute through the owning domain;
7. record outcome and residual exposure.

## Partial and Uncertain Effects

When a provider call times out or acknowledgement is lost, the runtime must reconcile with the authoritative system before retrying or compensating. Blind retries are prohibited where duplicate effects are possible.

## Manual Intervention

A saga enters `manual_intervention_required` when:

- compensation fails permanently;
- an irreversible effect produces an unacceptable outcome;
- authoritative state cannot be reconciled;
- required approval expires after partial completion;
- automated recovery would exceed delegated authority;
- a domain invariant blocks all safe next actions.

The intervention package must include a timeline, completed and unresolved effects, evidence references, current authoritative states, attempted recovery and recommended safe actions.

## Example Product Launch Saga

1. Confirm product acceptance.
2. Confirm brand and claims approval.
3. Authorize campaign budget.
4. Create campaign plan.
5. Publish through a governed provider adapter.
6. Observe launch outcome.

Possible compensation includes revoking unpublished assets, releasing unused budget and pausing distribution. Public impressions already delivered are mitigatable, not reversible.

## Invariants

- Every material forward step has an explicit reversibility classification.
- Compensation never rewrites another domain's history.
- Compensation produces new facts and audit evidence.
- Irreversible steps cannot rely on implied rollback.
- A compensated saga may still retain residual business or financial exposure, which must be recorded.
