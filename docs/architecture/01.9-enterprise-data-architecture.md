# 1.9 Enterprise Data Architecture

## Purpose

The Enterprise Data Architecture defines how ACOS governs, owns, classifies, contracts, stores, processes, protects, observes and retires data across business, platform, operations and intelligence domains.

## Principles

1. Data has an explicit business owner, technical owner and steward.
2. Schemas and contracts are versioned before producers or consumers change.
3. Sensitive data is minimized, classified and protected by default.
4. Lineage is recorded from authoritative source through transformation to consumer.
5. Data quality is measurable through declared service objectives.
6. Retention and deletion are policy-driven and auditable.
7. AI training, retrieval, memory and evaluation data remain traceable to approved sources.
8. Domains publish data products rather than exposing implementation-specific persistence.

## Data planes

- **Operational plane**: transactional state, workflow state, commands, projections and event stores.
- **Analytical plane**: governed warehouse, lakehouse, metrics, reporting and experimentation datasets.
- **Knowledge plane**: documents, metadata, embeddings, vector indexes, semantic models and knowledge graphs.
- **Control plane**: catalogs, contracts, lineage, quality rules, retention, legal holds and access policies.

## Canonical lifecycle

Data moves through `proposed`, `active`, `deprecated`, `archived` and `retired` states. Every active data product must declare ownership, classification, authoritative source, storage class, contract version, quality objectives, retention and consumers.

## Ownership model

The producing domain owns semantic correctness and contract evolution. Platform teams own shared data infrastructure. Security and governance authorities define mandatory controls. Consumers may not redefine producer-owned meaning through hidden copies.

## Data products and contracts

A data product is a governed interface over data. Its contract includes identity, schema version, compatibility mode, producer, consumers, quality objectives, latency, retention, access policy and lineage references.

Breaking changes require a new major version, migration plan, consumer acknowledgement and deprecation window. Additive compatible changes may use a minor version.

## Classification

The canonical classifications are:

- `public`
- `internal`
- `confidential`
- `restricted`

Classification determines access, encryption, logging, masking, export, retention and deletion controls.

## Quality and observability

Each active product defines measurable thresholds for completeness, validity, uniqueness, freshness and availability. Failures emit operational signals, identify affected consumers and invoke a remediation owner.

## Lineage

Lineage covers source, transformations, storage locations, materialized derivatives, downstream consumers, model or agent usage, archive and deletion. Derived AI artifacts must retain references to approved source data and transformation versions.

## Storage architecture

Storage technology follows workload characteristics rather than domain preference. Supported classes include transactional databases, event stores, analytical warehouses, object storage, caches, vector stores, graph stores and time-series stores. The registry records the logical storage class without binding architecture policy to a specific vendor.

## Privacy, security and compliance

Restricted and confidential data require encryption in transit and at rest. Personal data requires a lawful purpose, minimization, retention policy and deletion mechanism. Production data may not be copied into non-production environments without an approved masking or synthetic-data control.

## AI data controls

Training, retrieval, prompt, memory, evaluation and feedback datasets must declare provenance, intended use, prohibited use, sensitivity, retention and review authority. Model outputs do not become authoritative records without domain validation.

## Continuity

Critical products define recovery point and recovery time objectives, backup class, restore verification and regional continuity expectations. Recovery controls must be tested and evidenced.

## Governance

The machine-readable source of truth is `config/data/registry.yaml`, validated against `schemas/data-registry.schema.json` by `scripts/validate_data_registry.py` in CI.
