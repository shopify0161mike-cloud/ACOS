# 1.4 Enterprise Governance Architecture

## Purpose

This chapter defines the control system that constrains enterprise decisions, automated execution and material business actions across ACOS. Governance is implemented as executable policy, explicit authority, bounded risk, budget control, approval evidence and immutable auditability.

## Governance principles

1. Material actions are denied unless policy, authority, risk and budget checks succeed.
2. Policy evaluation is deterministic, versioned and reproducible.
3. Approval authority is explicit, scoped, time-bound and non-transferable.
4. Segregation of duties applies to high-impact and irreversible actions.
5. Risk acceptance requires a named accountable owner and expiry date.
6. Budget reservations occur before external spend or commitment.
7. Every decision produces evidence suitable for audit and replay.
8. Missing, stale or conflicting governance data causes fail-closed behavior.

## Governance control plane

The control plane contains six canonical functions:

- Policy decision: evaluates rules against subject, action, resource and context.
- Authorization: confirms that the actor may request or approve the action.
- Risk control: assigns inherent and residual risk and determines required treatment.
- Budget control: reserves, commits, releases and reconciles funds.
- Approval orchestration: collects required decisions without bypassing separation rules.
- Audit evidence: records the inputs, versions, outcomes and execution linkage.

## Decision sequence

A governed command follows this order:

1. Establish identity and execution context.
2. Resolve the canonical action and governed resource.
3. Load the applicable policy set and authority grants.
4. Classify financial, operational, legal, privacy, security and brand risk.
5. Determine required approvals and segregation constraints.
6. Reserve budget where financial commitment is possible.
7. Issue a time-bound governance decision token.
8. Execute through the enterprise execution architecture.
9. Reconcile outcome, release unused budget and append audit evidence.

No runtime service may reinterpret a denied governance decision as approval.

## Policy model

Policies have a stable identifier, owner, version, lifecycle state, scope, priority and deterministic effect. A policy may allow, deny, require approval, require evidence, impose limits or mandate compensating controls. Explicit deny overrides allow. Conflicts that cannot be resolved by priority are denied and escalated.

## Authority model

Authority grants bind a subject or role to permitted actions, resource scope, monetary limits, environments and validity periods. Delegation is allowed only when the originating grant explicitly permits it. Emergency authority is short-lived, independently reviewed and never suppresses audit requirements.

## Risk model

Risk is evaluated using likelihood and impact, producing an inherent rating. Controls reduce this to residual risk. Residual risk above tolerance requires treatment, exception approval or rejection. Exceptions are temporary records with owners, rationale, compensating controls and expiry.

## Budget model

Financial governance uses reservation before commitment. A reservation is associated with a command, cost center, currency, amount, expiry and policy decision. Commitments may not exceed the reservation. Partial consumption is reconciled and the remainder released.

## Approval model

Approval requirements are derived from policy and risk, not embedded in business services. Approval chains support role requirements, quorum, sequence, monetary thresholds, domain ownership and segregation of duties. Self-approval is prohibited where the requestor and approver roles conflict.

## Evidence and audit

Each decision record includes policy versions, actor identity, authority grants, evaluated facts, risk result, budget state, approvers, timestamps, decision reason, correlation identifiers and execution result. Evidence is append-only and retained according to classification and legal requirements.

## Governance invariants

- An expired decision token cannot authorize execution.
- A revoked authority grant cannot approve new actions.
- A budget commitment cannot exist without a valid reservation or an approved emergency exception.
- High-risk irreversible actions require independent approval.
- Exceptions cannot be permanent.
- Runtime components cannot mutate policy definitions.
- Audit evidence cannot be altered by the actor whose action it records.

## Integration boundaries

Governance consumes identity, command, domain ownership, execution and event contracts. It publishes decision, approval, risk, budget and exception events. Business domains remain owners of business facts; governance owns the decision about whether an action may proceed.
