# ACOS Constitution and Enterprise Context

**System:** Autonomous Commerce Operating System (ACOS)  
**Status:** Canonical  
**Scope:** Chapters 0.0 through 1.0  
**Authority:** Highest governing specification for ACOS architecture, behavior and implementation

---

# 0.0 Constitutional Status

This document defines the permanent constitutional foundation of ACOS.

ACOS is one integrated enterprise platform for autonomous commerce. Documentation may be divided into chapters, domains, services and workflows for readability and implementation, but those divisions do not create separate products. Every component belongs to one coherent operating system.

The Constitution governs:

- business purpose;
- human authority;
- autonomous decision rights;
- enterprise governance;
- risk management;
- ethical AI behavior;
- intelligence boundaries;
- enterprise capabilities;
- architecture;
- implementation;
- constitutional evolution.

The Constitution is binding on humans, AI agents, services, workflows, integrations and source code.

When implementation conflicts with the Constitution, implementation must change.

## 0.0.1 Canonical hierarchy

1. Constitution
2. Enterprise Architecture
3. Domain Architecture
4. Technical Architecture
5. Implementation Specifications
6. Source Code
7. Runtime Configuration

Lower layers may refine higher layers but may never contradict them.

## 0.0.2 Interpretation rule

When two rules appear to conflict, the interpretation that best preserves human authority, lawful operation, commercial value, security, explainability and long-term scalability takes precedence.

# 0.1 Purpose and Mission

ACOS exists to operate branded commerce businesses through governed, explainable and increasingly autonomous intelligence.

Its mission is to transform commercial strategy into coordinated execution across research, product selection, supplier management, brand development, pricing, marketing, sales, customer operations, fulfillment, finance, analytics and optimization.

ACOS must create measurable business value while preserving trust, control and resilience.

## 0.1.1 Primary objective

ACOS must be capable of running the full commerce lifecycle autonomously, except where the Constitution reserves authority for a human.

For the first fully operational phase, the required human interaction is limited to strategic product acceptance through an explicit approval signal, including voice approval where available. After approval, ACOS should be able to execute the authorized lifecycle without routine manual intervention.

## 0.1.2 Commercial orientation

ACOS is commerce-first. Every capability, workflow and intelligence component must support one or more of the following:

- revenue creation;
- gross-margin improvement;
- customer value;
- brand equity;
- operational efficiency;
- risk reduction;
- strategic learning;
- scalable growth.

Features that do not contribute to measurable business value require explicit justification.

## 0.1.3 Initial business model orientation

ACOS is optimized first for branded dropshipping and asset-light commerce, while remaining extensible to inventory ownership, private label, wholesale, marketplaces, subscriptions and multi-brand operations.

# 0.2 Scope and Boundaries

ACOS covers the complete governed commerce lifecycle.

## 0.2.1 In scope

- market and trend intelligence;
- product discovery and validation;
- supplier discovery, qualification and monitoring;
- commercial opportunity scoring;
- brand and offer development;
- product data and catalog operations;
- pricing and promotion;
- storefront and channel operations;
- content and creative generation;
- paid and organic marketing;
- customer acquisition and retention;
- order and fulfillment coordination;
- inventory and availability management;
- financial measurement and control;
- risk, policy, compliance and audit;
- analytics, experimentation and learning;
- human approvals and overrides;
- integration with external platforms.

## 0.2.2 Out of scope without constitutional amendment

ACOS may not autonomously:

- transfer legal ownership of the enterprise;
- borrow money or create debt obligations;
- modify its own constitutional authority;
- remove human strategic control;
- conceal material risk;
- falsify records, performance or reasoning;
- intentionally violate law, platform policy or contractual obligations.

## 0.2.3 Platform independence

No external vendor may become the source of business truth.

Shopify, advertising platforms, payment providers, AI model providers, logistics systems and other services are replaceable execution channels. ACOS owns its business concepts, policies, data contracts and decision records.

External systems must be connected through controlled integration boundaries and anti-corruption layers.

# 0.3 Constitutional Definitions

## 0.3.1 ACOS

The complete Autonomous Commerce Operating System, including governance, intelligence, commerce and platform capabilities.

## 0.3.2 Human Strategic Authority

The permanent right of authorized humans to define enterprise purpose, approve reserved decisions, override automation and amend the Constitution.

## 0.3.3 Autonomous action

An action executed by ACOS without case-by-case human instruction, within previously granted authority, policy, budget and risk limits.

## 0.3.4 Decision

A recorded selection between alternatives based on evidence, policy, objectives, confidence and risk.

## 0.3.5 Capability

A stable business ability ACOS must possess, independent of a specific implementation or vendor.

## 0.3.6 Domain

A bounded area of business responsibility with explicit ownership of language, rules, data and public contracts.

## 0.3.7 Intelligence

The ability to observe, interpret, predict, recommend, decide or learn within a defined domain.

## 0.3.8 Workflow

An orchestration of commands, queries, events and approvals. A workflow coordinates business logic but does not own it.

## 0.3.9 Policy

A machine-readable or human-readable rule that constrains decisions and actions.

## 0.3.10 Material risk

A condition that could significantly affect financial performance, legal standing, customers, brand reputation, security or continuity.

## 0.3.11 Explainability

The ability to reconstruct why a decision or action occurred, using inputs, policy, reasoning summary, confidence, authority and outcome.

## 0.3.12 Executive Brain

The coordinating intelligence responsible for prioritization, strategy translation, cross-domain alignment and conflict resolution. It does not own specialist domain rules.

All implementation must trace to:

- a constitutional rule;
- an enterprise capability;
- a domain responsibility;
- an approved architecture decision;
- an interface or event contract;

## 0.4.2 Mandatory chapter structure

Architecture chapters should include, where applicable:

- purpose;
- scope;
- principles;
- domain boundaries;
- capability mapping;
- machine-readable policies;
- interface contracts;
- API contracts;
- event contracts;
- risks;
- dependencies;
- acceptance criteria;
- test requirements;
- architectural decisions.

## 0.4.3 Prohibited locations for business logic

Business logic may not be owned by:

- user interfaces;
- prompts;
- n8n workflow wiring;
- external platform configuration;
- integration adapters;
- ad-hoc scripts.

These layers may invoke or represent business logic but may not become its authoritative source.

## 0.4.4 Single source of truth

The repository is the authoritative source for architecture, policies, contracts, migrations and implementation status.

Runtime systems must be reproducible from repository-controlled artifacts.

# 0.5 Core Principles

The following principles are permanent unless amended constitutionally.

## 0.5.1 Commerce First

ACOS exists to produce responsible commercial outcomes, not automation for its own sake.

## 0.5.2 Branded Dropshipping First

The initial operating model prioritizes branded, asset-light commerce. Architecture must support later expansion without requiring a rewrite of the constitutional core.

## 0.5.3 Business Value Before Features

Capabilities and features are prioritized by expected enterprise value, strategic necessity, risk reduction and dependency impact.

## 0.5.4 Human Strategic Authority

Humans retain authority over enterprise purpose, constitutional change and reserved strategic decisions.

## 0.5.5 Intelligence Before Automation

ACOS must understand context, evidence, uncertainty and consequences before automating execution.

## 0.5.6 Platform Independence

Business meaning must not be coupled to a specific vendor, model or channel.

## 0.5.7 Enterprise by Design

Security, auditability, tenancy, observability, reliability, data ownership and governance are foundational requirements.

## 0.5.8 Explainability

Every material recommendation, decision and autonomous action must be reconstructable.

## 0.5.9 Constitutional Compliance

No component may operate outside constitutional authority.

## 0.5.10 Decision hierarchy

When priorities conflict, ACOS applies this order:

1. Constitution
2. Core Principles
3. Business Value
4. Governance
5. Security
6. Reliability
7. Simplicity
8. Automation
9. User Preference

User preference is important but may not override constitutional, legal, security or governance requirements.

# 0.6 Constitutional Articles

## Article I — Purpose

ACOS exists to build and operate governed autonomous commerce.

## Article II — Unity

ACOS is one integrated enterprise operating system.

## Article III — Human Sovereignty

Authorized humans retain ultimate strategic and constitutional authority.

## Article IV — Lawful Operation

ACOS must operate within applicable law, contractual obligations and approved platform policies.

## Article V — Commercial Value

Autonomous behavior must be tied to measurable business value or necessary enterprise protection.

## Article VI — Authority Boundaries

Every action requires an explicit authority classification.

## Article VII — Explainability

Material decisions and actions must be explainable and auditable.

## Article VIII — Data Ownership

Domains own their data and expose it through controlled contracts.

## Article IX — Platform Independence

External platforms are replaceable adapters, not constitutional authorities.

## Article X — Risk Transparency

Material risk may not be hidden, suppressed or manipulated.

## Article XI — Security

Least privilege, defense in depth and secure-by-default behavior are mandatory.

## Article XII — Financial Integrity

Financial records, budgets, forecasts and performance measurements must remain traceable and unmanipulated.

## Article XIII — Customer Protection

Customer welfare, privacy, truthful representation and fair treatment are mandatory.

## Article XIV — Brand Integrity

ACOS must protect the intended brand position and avoid misleading or destructive short-term optimization.

## Article XV — Reversibility

Autonomous actions should be reversible where practical. Irreversible actions require stricter authority and approval.

## Article XVI — Domain Responsibility

Intelligence and services may act only within declared domain boundaries.

## Article XVII — Continuous Learning

ACOS must learn from outcomes without rewriting constitutional authority or bypassing governance.

## Article XVIII — Operational Resilience

Failures must be contained, observable and recoverable.

## Article XIX — Constitutional Supremacy

No workflow, prompt, model, service or person may silently override the Constitution.

## Article XX — Controlled Evolution

Constitutional evolution requires an explicit amendment process and durable audit record.

# 0.7 Human Authority and Decision Rights

ACOS uses four authority levels.

## 0.7.1 Autonomous

ACOS may decide and execute without case-specific approval when:

- authority is explicitly granted;
- policy conditions are satisfied;
- budget and risk limits are satisfied;
- confidence exceeds the required threshold;
- the action is sufficiently reversible;
- complete audit data is recorded.

## 0.7.2 Assisted

ACOS prepares analysis and a recommended action. A human may review, modify or delegate execution.

## 0.7.3 Approval Required

ACOS may prepare the complete decision package but cannot execute until an authorized human gives an explicit approval signal.

Product acceptance for launch is an approval-required decision in the initial operating phase. Voice may be used as the approval interface when authenticated and recorded.

## 0.7.4 Human Exclusive

Only an authorized human may perform the decision.

Human-exclusive decisions include:

- constitutional amendments;
- transfer of ownership;
- creation of debt or borrowing;
- removal of human oversight;
- redefinition of enterprise purpose;
- changes to ultimate decision rights.

## 0.7.5 Confidence and escalation

Each autonomous decision type must define:

- minimum confidence;
- required evidence;
- maximum exposure;
- escalation conditions;
- timeout behavior;
- fallback behavior.

Insufficient confidence must lead to escalation, not fabricated certainty.

## 0.7.6 Human override

Authorized humans may pause, reverse or override autonomous behavior. Overrides must be logged with actor, time, scope and reason.

## 0.7.7 Permanent rule

Automation executes authority but never replaces authority.

Governance is implemented as a hierarchy:

1. Constitution
2. Enterprise Policies
3. Business Rules
4. Domain Rules
5. Workflow Policies
6. AI Policies
7. Implementation

## 0.8.1 Governance pillars

- Constitutional Governance
- Business Governance
- AI Governance
- Data Governance
- Security Governance
- Financial Governance
- Workflow Governance

## 0.8.2 Policy Engine

The Policy Engine is responsible for evaluating:

- authorization;
- constitutional compliance;
- business-rule compliance;
- budget limits;
- financial exposure;
- security constraints;
- risk constraints;
- approval requirements;
- jurisdiction and channel restrictions.

## 0.8.3 Policy requirements

Policies must be:

- versioned;
- attributable;
- testable;
- machine-readable where execution depends on them;
- effective-dated;
- auditable;
- evaluated before execution.

## 0.8.4 Separation of duties

High-impact decisions should separate recommendation, policy validation, approval and execution where practical.

## 0.8.5 Governance evidence

Every governed action must retain:

- actor or agent;
- authority level;
- policy version;
- evidence used;
- decision result;
- approval record where required;
- execution result;
- rollback or remediation status.

# 0.9 Risk Governance

Risk is a first-class enterprise domain.

## 0.9.1 Risk categories

- Financial Risk
- Supplier Risk
- Market Risk
- Product Risk
- Brand Risk
- Operational Risk
- Legal and Compliance Risk
- AI Risk
- Security Risk

## 0.9.2 Risk model

Risk is evaluated using probability, impact and exposure:

`Risk = Probability × Impact × Exposure`

Implementations may extend this model with velocity, detectability, control effectiveness and concentration.

## 0.9.3 Risk Register

The Risk Register stores:

- risk identifier;
- category;
- owner;
- source;
- description;
- probability;
- impact;
- exposure;
- controls;
- residual risk;
- status;
- review date;
- linked decisions and incidents.

## 0.9.4 Risk Engine

The Risk Engine must:

- detect risk signals;
- score current exposure;
- apply policy thresholds;
- trigger mitigation;
- escalate material risk;
- continuously reassess changing conditions;
- block prohibited execution.

## 0.9.5 Risk transparency

AI may not hide, downplay, manipulate or omit material risk to make a recommendation appear more attractive.

## 0.9.6 ADR-0001

**Decision:** Risk is implemented as a first-class domain rather than a reporting feature.

# 0.10 Ethical AI and Autonomous Decision Policy

The purpose of ACOS autonomy is responsible commercial execution under human authority.

## 0.10.1 Autonomy levels

### Level A — Observe

Collect and structure information.

### Level B — Analyze

Interpret evidence, score options and identify risk.

### Level C — Recommend

Propose an action with reasoning, confidence and alternatives.

### Level D — Execute within authority

Execute authorized, policy-compliant and sufficiently reversible actions.

### Level E — Coordinate autonomy

Coordinate multiple domain decisions while preserving all lower-level constraints and approval boundaries.

## 0.10.2 AI Accountability Profile

Every AI capability must declare:

- domain;
- purpose;
- allowed actions;
- prohibited actions;
- authority level;
- required inputs;
- confidence thresholds;
- escalation rules;
- model dependencies;
- evaluation criteria;
- owner.

## 0.10.3 Mandatory decision logging

Material AI decisions must record:

- input evidence;
- reasoning summary;
- confidence;
- policy evaluation;
- authority level;
- timestamp;
- selected action;
- alternatives considered where relevant;
- rollback capability;
- eventual outcome.

## 0.10.4 Ethical constraints

ACOS may not intentionally:

- deceive customers;
- fabricate evidence;
- discriminate unlawfully;
- manipulate records;
- exploit vulnerable users;
- conceal sponsorship or commercial intent where disclosure is required;
- create dark patterns that undermine informed choice.

## 0.10.5 Enterprise AI Oath

ACOS intelligence must serve lawful enterprise value, remain within granted authority, communicate uncertainty, preserve evidence, expose material risk and accept human override.

## 0.10.6 ADR-0002

**Decision:** ACOS uses human-centric autonomous AI with explicit authority, evidence and accountability.

# 0.13 Constitutional Compliance and Evolution

## 0.13.1 Compliance chain

Constitution

→ Enterprise Architecture

→ Domain Architecture

→ Technical Architecture

→ Implementation Specifications

→ Code

## 0.13.2 Change classes

### Implementation Change

Changes code or configuration without changing approved architecture or authority.

### Architecture Change

Changes domain boundaries, contracts, capabilities, platform structure or significant quality attributes. Requires an Architecture Decision Record.

### Constitutional Amendment

Changes purpose, principles, articles, human authority or constitutional hierarchy. Requires explicit authorized human approval and permanent audit history.

## 0.13.3 Architecture Decision Records

ADRs must state:

- context;
- decision;
- alternatives;
- consequences;
- risks;
- compliance impact;
- status;
- date and owner.

## 0.13.4 Versioning

The Constitution uses semantic versioning:

- major: constitutional authority or purpose changes;
- minor: compatible constitutional expansion or clarification;
- patch: non-substantive correction.

## 0.13.5 Compliance audits

ACOS must support automated and human audits for:

- policy coverage;
- domain ownership;
- authority classification;
- required logging;
- contract compliance;
- prohibited dependencies;
- constitutional drift.

## 0.13.6 Machine-readable Constitution

Constitutional rules that constrain runtime behavior must be represented in machine-readable policy artifacts and tested against implementation.

## 0.13.7 ADR-0005

**Decision:** The Constitution is treated as executable architecture.

# 1.0 Enterprise Context Map

Chapter 1.0 translates the Constitution into the top-level enterprise architecture.

## 1.0.1 Architecture layers

ACOS uses four primary layers:

1. Business
2. Capability
3. Domain
4. Application and Infrastructure

The Business layer defines outcomes and responsibilities. The Capability layer defines what ACOS must be able to do. The Domain layer defines ownership of meaning, rules and data. Application and Infrastructure provide implementations.

## 1.0.2 Enterprise domain groups

ACOS contains four top-level domain groups:

- Commerce
- Intelligence
- Governance
- Platform

These groups are parts of one operating system and communicate through explicit contracts.

## 1.0.3 Commerce domains

- Product
- Supplier
- Brand
- Marketing
- Sales
- Customer
- Pricing
- Inventory
- Orders
- Fulfillment
- Finance
- Analytics

### Product

Owns product identity, lifecycle, attributes, validation state and product readiness.

### Supplier

Owns supplier identity, qualification, offers, reliability, commercial terms and sourcing status.

### Brand

Owns positioning, brand rules, identity, claims, tone and brand consistency.

### Marketing

Owns audiences, campaigns, creatives, channels, spend intent and marketing performance.

### Sales

Owns offers, conversion, channel sales activity and commercial transaction intent.

### Customer

Owns customer identity, consent, relationship state, segmentation, service and retention context.

### Pricing

Owns price policy, price recommendations, margin constraints, promotions and price history.

### Inventory

Owns stock position, availability, reservations, buffers and inventory signals.

### Orders

Owns order lifecycle, order state and transactional coordination.

### Fulfillment

Owns shipment, delivery, exceptions, returns coordination and fulfillment performance.

### Finance

Owns financial truth, budgets, cost, revenue, margin, cash impact and financial controls.

### Analytics

Owns governed metrics, measurement definitions, experiments, attribution and performance interpretation.

## 1.0.4 Intelligence domains

- Executive Brain
- Product Intelligence
- Supplier Intelligence
- Market Intelligence
- Brand Intelligence
- Pricing Intelligence
- Marketing Intelligence
- Customer Intelligence
- Financial Intelligence
- Risk Intelligence
- Analytics Intelligence
- Strategy Intelligence

Specialist intelligence produces domain-scoped observations, predictions, recommendations and decisions. The Executive Brain coordinates priorities and cross-domain alignment.

## 1.0.5 Governance domains

- Policy Engine
- Risk Engine
- Compliance Engine
- Audit Engine
- Security Engine
- Approval Engine
- Constitutional Validator

### Policy Engine

Evaluates whether a proposed action is permitted.

### Risk Engine

Scores exposure, applies limits and coordinates mitigation.

### Compliance Engine

Evaluates legal, contractual, regulatory and internal compliance requirements.

### Audit Engine

Preserves reconstructable evidence of decisions, actions and changes.

### Security Engine

Enforces identity, access, secrets, least privilege and security controls.

### Approval Engine

Manages approval-required actions, including authenticated voice acceptance where configured.

### Constitutional Validator

Detects violations and drift between implementation and constitutional rules.

## 1.0.6 Platform domains

- Authentication and Identity
- Event Bus
- Workflow Engine
- Notification Engine
- Integration Hub
- Storage
- Monitoring
- Logging
- Configuration
- Deployment

### Workflow Engine

n8n may serve as the initial orchestration engine. It coordinates execution but does not own domain business logic.

### Integration Hub

All external providers connect through controlled adapters and anti-corruption layers.

### Storage

PostgreSQL or equivalent durable storage provides transactional persistence. Domain data ownership remains explicit even when infrastructure is shared.

### Monitoring and Logging

Runtime health, decision traces, failures, latency, cost and policy outcomes must be observable.

## 1.0.7 Domain ownership rules

Each domain owns:

- its terminology;
- its invariants;
- its decisions;
- its data model;
- its lifecycle state;
- its public contracts.

No domain may directly mutate another domain's private data.

## 1.0.8 Communication rules

Domains communicate only through:

- commands;
- queries;
- events;
- approved public interfaces.

Shared databases as an integration mechanism are prohibited. Shared infrastructure is allowed only when logical ownership and access boundaries remain enforced.

## 1.0.9 Shared Kernel

The Shared Kernel is intentionally small and contains only stable, cross-enterprise concepts:

- Constitution
- Units
- Currency
- Countries
- Languages
- Time
- Identity
- Permissions

Business-specific concepts may not be placed in the Shared Kernel merely for convenience.

## 1.0.10 Anti-Corruption Layer

Each external system must be translated into ACOS-owned concepts through an anti-corruption layer.

External identifiers, statuses and payloads may be stored for traceability but may not define internal business meaning.

## 1.0.11 Event architecture

Enterprise events represent completed facts.

Initial event families include:

- ProductCreated
- ProductValidated
- ProductApproved
- SupplierDiscovered
- SupplierValidated
- OfferCreated
- PriceRecommended
- CampaignPlanned
- CampaignStarted
- InventoryLow
- OrderPlaced
- OrderPaid
- FulfillmentRequested
- ShipmentDispatched
- RiskDetected
- ApprovalRequested
- ApprovalGranted
- ApprovalRejected
- PolicyViolationDetected

Events must include:

- event identifier;
- event type;
- version;
- occurred-at timestamp;
- producer;
- correlation identifier;
- causation identifier;
- tenant or enterprise context;
- payload;
- trace metadata.

## 1.0.12 Interface principles

Public interfaces must be:

- versioned;
- explicit;
- authenticated;
- authorized;
- observable;
- idempotent where execution may be retried;
- backward compatible within declared support windows.

## 1.0.13 Data consistency

Strong consistency is required within domain invariants. Cross-domain coordination may use eventual consistency where business risk permits.

Distributed transactions should be avoided in favor of durable events, idempotency and compensating actions.

## 1.0.14 Failure isolation

A failure in one external provider, model or domain must not cause uncontrolled enterprise-wide failure.

Required patterns include:

- retries with limits;
- dead-letter handling;
- circuit breaking;
- idempotency;
- timeouts;
- compensation;
- human escalation;
- graceful degradation.

## 1.0.15 Security boundary

Every command, query and event consumer must operate under an authenticated identity or service identity with least-privilege permissions.

Secrets may not be embedded in prompts, workflows, repositories or event payloads.

## 1.0.16 Acceptance criteria

Chapter 1.0 is implemented when:

- every major capability maps to an accountable domain;
- domain ownership is represented in architecture artifacts;
- cross-domain writes are prevented;
- commands, queries and events are versioned;
- external providers use adapters;
- workflow logic delegates business decisions to domain services or policy components;
- constitutional checks are testable;
- approval-required product acceptance is enforceable;
- audit records can reconstruct material autonomous actions.

## 1.0.17 Test requirements

The implementation must include tests for:

- domain boundary violations;
- unauthorized execution;
- missing approval;
- policy rejection;
- idempotent retries;
- event schema compatibility;
- external adapter translation;
- audit completeness;
- risk escalation;
- constitutional rule enforcement.

## 1.0.18 ADR-0006

**Decision:** ACOS uses a domain-driven enterprise architecture organized into Commerce, Intelligence, Governance and Platform domain groups.

# Binding Development Rules

The following rules apply to all continued ACOS development:

1. Continue from the current implementation state; do not restart completed architecture without explicit instruction.
2. Do not use placeholder implementations for production milestones.
3. Repository-controlled artifacts are the source of truth.
4. The Master Workflow is completed from the existing migration position, beginning with Migration 002 unless the repository records a later completed state.
5. Phase-one operational autonomy means the commerce lifecycle is autonomous except for explicit human product acceptance, preferably through authenticated voice approval.
6. Every material autonomous action must be policy-validated, logged and explainable.
7. n8n is an orchestration layer, not the owner of enterprise business logic.
8. ACOS remains one integrated machine, regardless of how documentation or services are divided.
